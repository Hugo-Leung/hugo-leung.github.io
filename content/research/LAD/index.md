### Overview

The LAD experiment in Hall C performs tagged deep inelastic scattering (DIS) on deuterium to study how the structure of neutrons is modified inside the nuclear medium. By detecting backward spectator protons in coincidence with the scattered electron, the experiment isolates interactions on high‑momentum, highly‑virtual neutrons—configurations closely tied to short‑range correlations (SRCs). This approach provides a controlled way to probe the origin of the EMC effect and to test models of nucleon modification in nuclei.


### LAD Detector Stack

The recoil‑proton detection system consists of refurbished scintillator planes arranged to provide wide angular acceptance for backward‑moving protons. These planes work together with two large-area GEM tracking detectors placed close to the target for precise vertexing and trajectory reconstruction. The combined system enables efficient identification of spectator protons and suppression of background in high‑rate conditions.

### Detector Operations and Tracking Support

My contributions to LAD focus on the preparation and support of the GEM tracking detectors used for vertexing and trajectory reconstruction near the target. I take part in commissioning these detectors, performing calibrations, checking data quality, and verifying stable operation in the high‑rate environment expected in Hall C. I also serve as an on‑call expert for the GEM system, assisting with real-time diagnostics, troubleshooting, and ensuring consistent, reliable detector performance during running periods.

In addition to detector operations, I help develop and refine analysis procedures for GEM-based tracking and its integration into the LAD reconstruction workflow. These efforts support LAD's ability to accurately identify spectator protons and achieve the precision needed for its physics goals.
