## Abstract

We report on the commissioning and cosmic characterization of the Gas Electron Multiplier (GEM) trackers for the recently completed first round of the Super Bigbite Spectrometer (SBS) experimental program conducted in Hall A of the Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility. The SBS GEM trackers rank as one of the largest deployments of triple GEM detectors in operation and were critical to the success of the SBS program. Categorized by their readout strip orientation, the 56 individual SBS GEM modules range in active areas from 60&nbsp;cm\(\times\)50&nbsp;cm (XY) to 150&nbsp;cm\(\times\)40&nbsp;cm (UV, XW) and were fabricated at the University of Virginia. Large area layers were assembled from four XY GEM modules or a single UV or XW GEM module. The front-end electronics were based on APV25 chips with output sent to custom developed 40&nbsp;MHz Multi-purpose Digitizers (MPDs). Prior to the installation of the GEM-based tracking systems for SBS experiments, the GEM modules underwent extensive cosmic-ray testing to characterize the performance of each module, including detector stability, efficiency, uniformity, timing performance, and spatial resolution.
